# Streamer incident clip

`streamer-incident.mp4` is an 87.303-second, 1920x1080 H.264/AAC clip supplied for this investigation.

The supplied file used a Matroska container with an `.mp4` extension. I remuxed it without re-encoding into a browser-compatible MP4 and removed its container metadata.

## Integrity

- Supplied artifact SHA-256: `559AB57D3B6B75C370053281307C437B44E54BAD577150D1CBE04613CCFED745`
- Normalized MP4 SHA-256: `E15F23A9B6586DA0EFF7FFBE919D9EC08FC3A30938743B1C362D467E67D0C22E`

## What the clip shows

- Sequential promotional messages, including a Discord invitation and social-media promotion, rendered as colored native-looking world-space dialogue above a nearby NPC.
- The NPC remains near the player while the player moves away along the road.

## What the clip does not establish

- It does not show the reported online-balance change.
- It does not expose the remote peer's SteamID.
- It does not establish whether the peer joined through an open lobby, an invite path, or a direct transport connection.
- It does not identify the exact RPC or independently prove server-side NPC target manipulation.
